Task 1 — Proposal coverage audit

“Covered” means the draft addresses the requirement. “Partial” means part of the requirement is missing or the available evidence is incomplete. This is a document comparison. Source verification and code review are later tasks.

1. Title, authors, and problem — Covered. The title and author line identify Jay Patel and Sami Darouiche. Section 1 frames the comparison around performance, misuse, and the limits of a deliberately weak teaching cipher.
2. Implement AES-128-GCM, AES-256-GCM, and ChaCha20-Poly1305 — Covered. Section 3 names all three configurations and the cryptography classes used.
3. Design and implement a small educational cipher with a deliberately small keyspace — Covered. Section 3 explains Toy-XOR-16, its repeating key, and its educational purpose. Sections 3 and 6 identify the pure-Python comparison limit.
4. Benchmark encryption and decryption across different input sizes — Covered in the draft. Sections 2 and 4 specify the sizes, repetitions, and both operations.
5. Report encryption and decryption time — Partial. Section 2 describes timing, and Section 4 gives throughput and run-to-run ratios. Section 4 does not directly report elapsed encryption or decryption times for the benchmark configurations.
6. Report throughput — Covered in the draft. Section 4 gives separate encryption and decryption tables and a run-to-run variation table. The complete results needed to verify the aggregates were not supplied.
7. Measure memory where practical — Covered with a stated limitation. Section 4 reports sampled Python allocation peaks for both operations at the smallest and largest sizes. Total process memory remains unmeasured in the draft.
8. Perform brute force against the custom cipher — Covered in the draft. Section 5 reports whether the search recovered the key and gives the observed time range. The full run evidence for that range was not supplied.
9. Demonstrate repeated-block leakage with AES-ECB — Covered. Section 5 gives the observed distinct-block outcome for repeated input and identifies ECB as a separate demonstration.
10. Modify encrypted data and check whether authentication detects tampering — Covered. Section 5 reports the outcomes for ciphertext and tag changes, associated-data and nonce mismatches, and the toy-cipher comparison. It also mentions the wrong-key unit test.
11. Demonstrate consequences of nonce reuse — Covered. Section 5 reports the confidentiality result for all three standard configurations and limits the conclusion to the demonstrated outcome.
12. Examine basic diffusion — Covered. Section 5 describes the bit-flip experiment and gives separate ciphertext-body and tag results.
13. Examine output characteristics — Covered in the draft. Section 5 reports entropy, chi-square, and repeated-block observations. The extra experiment script and its result files were not supplied.
14. Evaluate security experiments by whether the intended effects were demonstrated — Covered. Section 5 reports outcomes per experiment. Section 6 limits the conclusions to the cases tested and identifies excluded areas.
15. Use Python 3, cryptography, NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib, perf_counter_ns(), and pytest — Partial in the draft. Sections 2 and 3 identify Python and the four libraries, and Section 2 names the timing function. Section 5 mentions unit tests without naming pytest. The ZIP includes pytest.ini and a test module.
16. Use controlled synthetic inputs and no confidential data — Covered, with a wording conflict. Section 2 says all inputs are synthetic random bytes. Section 5 also describes synthetic repeated-block inputs. Section 2 should distinguish random benchmark data from deliberately patterned security-test inputs.
17. Run locally in a controlled environment — Partial. Sections 2 and 6 identify the current cloud container and describe the benchmark protocol. The planned run on the team's computer is pending.
18. Record OS, CPU, Python version, cryptographic library version, test sizes, and repetition counts — Covered in Section 2 for the draft environment.
19. Address the expected findings — Covered for the stated experimental scope. Sections 4–6 discuss hardware-dependent performance, detection of the tested modifications, ECB leakage, the nonce-reuse result, the custom cipher's weaknesses, and the limits of judging security from ciphertext appearance. Section 6 treats the findings as experimental evidence.
20. Analyze results and create graphs — Partial. Section 4 analyzes performance and points to a combined throughput figure. The referenced combined figure is absent from the supplied ZIP. The ZIP contains sample encryption and decryption plots.
21. Complete analysis and final report — Partial. The draft has the main analysis and conclusion sections. The local rerun and final revisions are pending. The proposal's weekly timeline is a work plan, so reproducing it in the report is unnecessary. Actual completion dates cannot be inferred from the draft.
22. Carry over the proposal references and support the background — Partial. The draft lists all five proposal references and adds the cryptography documentation. The Webster and Tavares entry remains a placeholder with no specific point attributed to the paper. Citation accuracy and claim support will be checked in Task 3.
